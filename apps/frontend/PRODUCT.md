# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary users are enterprise knowledge workers researching, drafting, analyzing documents, and running approved workflows in the browser. They need to complete meaningful work with AI while retaining enough visibility and control to trust consequential agent actions, without having to understand the governance infrastructure underneath.

The secondary users are CTOs, CISOs, Heads of AI, and enterprise IT administrators. They configure approved models, skills, integrations, policies, and approvals; oversee adoption; and review activity when governance or operational questions arise.

## Product Purpose

Cogniplane provides one governed browser workspace for enterprise AI work. It replaces fragmented public chat tools, unmanaged local agents, and shadow connectors with company-approved capabilities, isolated sessions, explicit approvals, artifacts, and replayable activity.

Success means employees can complete useful knowledge work in one approachable workspace while the organization retains the controls and evidence required to operate AI agents responsibly.

## Positioning

Cogniplane makes powerful AI agents safe and practical for everyday enterprise work by combining a usable workspace with governance built into every action.

## Brand Personality

Assured, precise, and approachable. Cogniplane should communicate calm competence: serious enough for security and IT leadership, clear enough for everyday employees, and direct about what the system is doing. The interface should create confidence without becoming clinical, intimidating, or verbose.

## Anti-references

Cogniplane must not resemble a generic consumer chatbot whose identity stops at a transcript and prompt box. It must also avoid the feeling of complex enterprise software that exposes its internal architecture, governance model, or configuration burden before the user needs it.

## Design Principles

1. **Keep the work primary.** Research, writing, analysis, and artifacts should dominate the experience; platform machinery stays quiet until it becomes relevant.
2. **Make consequential actions legible.** When an agent needs approval or encounters a policy boundary, show what will happen, why attention is needed, and what the user can do next.
3. **Reveal complexity progressively.** Familiar controls and clear defaults serve everyday users, while deeper governance and runtime detail remain available to administrators in context.
4. **Earn confidence through evidence.** Status, history, approvals, and audit information should be specific and trustworthy rather than decorative or reassuring by assertion.
5. **Use one coherent product language.** Workspace, settings, and administration should share predictable interaction patterns so expertise transfers across the product.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support complete keyboard operation, visible focus states, meaningful screen-reader semantics, reduced-motion preferences, sufficient text and control contrast, and status communication that never relies on color alone. Dense administrative information should remain readable and operable at browser zoom and across responsive layouts.
